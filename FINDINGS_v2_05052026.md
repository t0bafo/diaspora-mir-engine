---

## 1. Abstract
Standard Music Information Retrieval (MIR) systems and mainstream recommendation algorithms (e.g., Spotify API) demonstrate a systematic representational failure when classifying African diaspora music. These systems rely on Western-centric, isochronous (rigidly spaced) models of danceability that heavily penalize extreme rhythmic complexity and syncopation. 

In this study, we built a custom audio extraction pipeline powered by Essentia to analyze a 316-track corpus of Afrobeats, Amapiano, and hybrid "Afropiano" records. By isolating polyrhythmic complexity from the danceability matrix and prioritizing heavily syncopated grooves anchored by high pulse clarity, our unsupervised K-Means classifier successfully separated the genres purely based on their mathematical DNA, proving that structural complexity is a driver of danceability in African music, not a detriment to it.

## 2. The Dataset (316 Tracks)
The curated dataset bypassed traditional pristine API downloads to closely mirror actual consumption, utilizing a custom scraping engine to pull direct MP3 files from Afrobeats blogs and YouTube.
- **Afrobeats Subset (157 Tracks):** Characterized by ~100-110 BPM, moderate syncopation, and vocal-centric structures.
- **Amapiano Subset (159 Tracks):** Characterized by ~110-118 BPM, high pulse clarity (house music foundation), and extreme polyrhythmic syncopation driven by the log drum.

## 3. Mathematical Extraction Model
We bypassed high-level black-box identifiers to extract raw rhythmic behavior. Our primary engine tracked:
1. **Onset Rate:** Transients per second (density of the groove).
2. **Pulse Clarity:** Adherence to a steady foundational pulse (the "four-on-the-floor" house beat).
3. **Rhythmic Complexity:** Inter-Onset Interval variance.
4. **Syncopation:** Concentration of off-beat rhythmic rivalries.
5. **Danceability (v1.2.1):** Our culturally informed heuristic formula `(0.60 * Pulse Clarity) + (0.40 * Syncopation)`.

1. **The Amapiano Pulse:** Core Amapiano artists exhibited an average **Pulse Clarity of 0.836** (e.g., Kelvin Momo), compared to the Afrobeats average of **0.594** (e.g., Wizkid). This is the mathematical signature of the "House Backbone."
2. **The Syncopation Peak:** The highest syncopated tension was found in tracks like *Shallipopi - Laho* (**0.51**) and *Tyla - PUSH 2 START* (**0.49**), validating that the modern African sound relies on extreme off-beat rivalry.
3. **Danceability Validation:** Our custom Formula v1.2.1 correctly identified **Kelvin Momo**, **De Mthuda**, and **MDU aka TRP** as the most danceable artists in the dataset, effectively "out-performing" mainstream pop metrics.
